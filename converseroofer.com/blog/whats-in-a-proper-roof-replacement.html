What's in a Proper Roof Replacement: 7 Layers Your Estimate Should List

A roof is more than shingles. Here are the seven layers a complete Converse roof replacement includes, what each one does, and how to read a roofing estimate line by line.

September 13, 2026 · 6 min read · Roof replacement · Guides

Two roofing estimates for the same house can be thousands of dollars apart and look almost identical on paper. The difference is usually what's missing from the cheaper one. Here are the seven layers a proper roof replacement in Converse includes, top to bottom, and why each one is on our written estimate.

1. Ridge vent and cap shingles

Hot attic air has to get out. A continuous ridge vent along the peak, paired with intake at the soffits, keeps attic temperatures down, which extends shingle life and lowers summer cooling bills. Cap shingles cover the vent and the hips. An estimate that says "ridge" without "vent" usually means the old turtle vents are going back on.

2. Shingles

The wear layer. Architectural (dimensional) shingles are the standard on Converse homes and carry 30-year to lifetime material warranties. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles use a more flexible asphalt blend and pass a 2-inch steel-ball drop test; here's when they're worth it. Your estimate should name the manufacturer, the product line and the color, not just "30-year shingle."

3. Starter strip and drip edge

Starter strip is a sealed first course along the eaves and rakes that stops wind from lifting the bottom row. Drip edge is the metal flashing along every edge that carries water off the deck and into the gutter instead of behind the fascia. Both are cheap, both are required by code in Texas, and both are commonly skipped by crews in a hurry.

4. Ice-and-water shield

A self-adhering rubberized membrane in the places water concentrates: valleys, around chimneys, skylights, pipe penetrations and along the eaves. It seals around nails. In Converse it's not about ice; it's about the two inches of rain that fell in an hour on September 11.

5. Synthetic underlayment

The second water barrier across the whole deck, under the shingles. Synthetic underlayment has replaced old felt paper because it doesn't tear, wrinkle or absorb water. If an estimate says "15 lb felt," it's an older spec.

6. Decking

The plywood or OSB the whole roof sits on. Nobody knows its condition until the old shingles come off, so a fair estimate prices decking replacement per sheet up front and shows you the soft or rotten sheets before replacing them. An estimate with no decking line is an estimate with a surprise in it.

7. Flashing, boots and vents

Step flashing where the roof meets walls, counter-flashing at the chimney, new pipe boots at every plumbing vent, and new roof vents. Reusing old flashing on a new roof is the most common cause of a leak in year two. Every one of these gets replaced on a Converse Roofer job and each is listed.

What else belongs on the estimate

  • Tear-off and disposal of the existing roof, including the number of layers.
  • Nailing spec. Six nails per shingle in our climate, not four.
  • Cleanup. Tarps over landscaping and a magnet sweep of the yard and driveway for nails.
  • Warranty. The manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty, in writing, with the company's local address.
  • Schedule and payment terms. When it starts, how long it takes, and when payment is due.

How to compare two estimates

Line them up layer by layer using the list above. If one is missing drip edge, ice-and-water, starter strip, ridge vent or new flashing, that's where the price difference went. Ask the cheaper bidder to add the missing lines and re-quote. The gap usually closes.
